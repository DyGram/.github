## Dynamics of Grammatical Stability (DyGram)

"Dynamics of Grammatical Stability" (DyGram) addresses the "actuation problem" of
language change by investigating why grammatical structures remain stable or undergo
change during linguistic transmission. While historical linguistics typically describes the
results of change, DyGram models the mechanisms of stability as a quantifiable state
of balance in linguistic input of learners. For the first time, we will study a comprehensive
1000-year French corpus and modern acquisition data in parallel to identify the historical
distributional tipping points where structures become unlearnable. By testing these
thresholds across both diachronic and developmental data, we determine if the same
quantitative principles that govern child language acquisition also dictate the long-term
stability of a language’s grammar.
DyGram is positioned at the critical intersection of general principles of language change
and purely empirical, frequency-based corpus analyses. While being data-driven, it is
committed to the theoretical link between acquisition and change, specifically by applying
computational models of learnability to both diachronic and acquisitional data. We move
beyond the observation of shifts to investigate the specific thresholds where grammatical
stability dissolves, treating stability not as a static default, but as a dynamic outcome of
learnable input.
